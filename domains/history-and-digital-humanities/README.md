# History and Digital Humanities

**Scope.** Quantitative and computational study of the human record: digitized texts, inscriptions, manuscripts, published archaeological datasets, chronology, stylometry, and undeciphered scripts.

**Why this is tractable without a lab.** Fieldwork, excavation, and new dating are out of reach, but an enormous published and digitized record is not, and no human scholar can read all of it. This domain accepts only claims that are falsifiable against that record.

## How results are verified here

- Every claim cites the specific document, edition, or dataset record
- Statistical claims (authorship, dating, networks) come with validation on cases where the answer is known
- Readings and translations give the source text alongside
- Published excavation, radiocarbon, and isotopic data are `external-data`: accepted as reported, not reproducible here

## Public data and reference sources

- Perseus, Papyri.info, Trismegistos
- Epigraphic Database Heidelberg, CDLI (cuneiform)
- Internet Archive, HathiTrust public domain, Wikisource
- Open Context, tDAR, published radiocarbon compilations
- Seshat global history databank

Sources are listed from general knowledge as starting points. Confirm current
access terms and versions before use, and record them in the project.

## Seed directions

These are suggestions, not a queue. Any well-posed question in scope is welcome.

- Stylometric authorship and dating studies with validated methods
- Cross-referencing dispersed sources to reconstruct prosopographies, itineraries, or price series
- Statistical structure of undeciphered scripts, rigorously separating what is established from what is speculation
- Bayesian chronological modelling over published radiocarbon dates
- Astronomical dating of historical records (eclipses, comets) with computed ephemerides

## Limits and cautions

- Interpretive, aesthetic, or moral judgements are outside scope; keep to checkable claims
- Decipherment claims are `conjecture` until they predict held-out material
- OCR and digitization errors are a first-class source of error; sample and check
